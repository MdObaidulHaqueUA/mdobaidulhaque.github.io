Focus Area

Research

Epidemic transmission is fundamentally heterogeneous: it unfolds through individual contact events along the specific social ties people maintain in daily life. My research is organized around a central question: how substantially do epidemic outcomes change once heterogeneous contact networks, context-dependent behavioral responses, and the intrinsic stochasticity of transmission are treated as primary features rather than computational approximations?

A core research thread situates epidemic dynamics on a contact network in which transmission requires direct proximity, while each agent's compliance behavior evolves in response to local epidemic conditions, perceived risk, and social influence. To model this behavioral heterogeneity with fidelity, large language models (LLMs) serve as behavioral engines: given an agent's demographic profile and the current epidemic state, the LLM generates a contextually appropriate protection decision. This design produces outbreak trajectories exhibiting stratified heterogeneity that classical homogeneous-compliance baselines cannot replicate.

The per-agent cost of live LLM inference at each simulation step motivates a complementary research thread: distilling the LLM's behavioral policy into a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) surrogate. Trained on 15,000 Latin Hypercube-sampled observations, the GAM reproduces the LLM's decision surface with 93.89% decision coincidence on held-out data, while eliminating all marginal API costs at runtime and making large-scale sensitivity analyses computationally tractable.

In parallel, a foundational theoretical thread develops rigorous stochastic representations of epidemic dynamics on finite networks. Martingale decompositions for the SIR process on both complete and sparse graphs yield exact characterizations of sample-path fluctuations that mean-field equations suppress. Large-population scaling results connect these finite-N processes to their ODE fluid limits, providing quantitative error bounds and a principled bridge from individual-level stochastic dynamics to population-level predictions.

Experimental Results · 2026

Key Research Findings

Three interconnected projects establish a rigorous stochastic framework for epidemic modeling with LLM-driven behavioral heterogeneity. Project I derives exact martingale decompositions and fluid-limit ODEs for a Poisson-clock SIR model on complete and sparse networks. Project II introduces a SEIRS agent-based framework in which GPT-4o-mini serves as the behavioral oracle, examining compliance heterogeneity across age, income, and occupation relative to classical baselines. Project III distills the LLM's decision policy into a Generalized Additive Model (GAM) surrogate that enables large-scale simulations at zero marginal API cost.

The LLM model's context-sensitive, time-varying compliance decisions, combined with waning immunity, generate recurrent epidemic waves absent from all non-LLM baselines.

What Comes Next

Future Research Directions

The three research threads described above open into a broader agenda centered on strengthening theoretical guarantees, validating behavioral realism against empirical evidence, and scaling the simulation framework to policy-relevant population sizes.

I

SIR & Martingale: Theoretical Extensions

  • Extend fluid-limit and pair-approximation results to heterogeneous networks with arbitrary degree distributions, including configuration models and power-law graphs.
  • Establish finite-N concentration inequalities via martingale bounds, quantifying the rate at which sample paths converge to the ODE fluid limit.
  • Generalize the six-compartment martingale framework to SEIRS dynamics, unifying Projects I and II under a common stochastic formulation.
  • Investigate multi-strain and seasonal extensions of the Poisson-clock model.
II

SEIRS-IPC-LLM: Behavioral Validation & Scale

  • Validate LLM-generated compliance decisions against empirical survey data from the Oxford COVID-19 Government Response Tracker and CDC behavioral surveillance studies.
  • Benchmark behavioral fidelity across state-of-the-art LLMs, including GPT-4o, GPT-4.1, Llama-3.1, and Mistral, to assess cross-model consistency.
  • Extend the framework to multi-pathogen and seasonal dynamics, incorporating co-circulating variants and heterologous immunity.
  • Introduce adaptive government policy as a feedback mechanism, wherein intervention intensity responds dynamically to epidemic state variables.
III

GAM Distillation: Surrogates & Policy Design

  • Benchmark GAM against neural surrogate alternatives (multilayer perceptron, random forest, gradient-boosted trees) on the interpretability–accuracy tradeoff.
  • Scale simulations to N = 50,000+ agents with full Monte Carlo sensitivity analyses, leveraging the surrogate's zero-marginal-cost inference.
  • Calibrate the distilled framework against real outbreak records from COVID-19 and seasonal influenza to assess retrospective forecasting accuracy.
  • Apply the surrogate to optimal intervention design, searching systematically over policy parameters to minimize epidemic burden.

The long-term objective is an open-source, calibrated epidemic simulation toolkit that integrates rigorous stochastic theory with LLM-driven behavioral realism, enabling reproducible and policy-relevant epidemic modeling at realistic population scales.
