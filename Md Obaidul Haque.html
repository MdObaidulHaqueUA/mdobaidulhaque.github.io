Focus Area

Research

My work sits at the intersection of stochastic processes, network theory, and computational epidemiology. The central question driving my research is: how do individual-level behavioral decisions, heterogeneous contact networks, and stochastic noise collectively shape the course of infectious disease outbreaks?

To answer this, I develop hybrid agent-based epidemic models (HABEMs) in which each node of a contact network is an autonomous agent whose behavioral state is updated by a fine-tuned large language model. I complement this computational program with rigorous mathematical analysis — martingale decompositions, pair approximations, and moment-closure hierarchies — that quantify finite-size corrections and network effects beyond mean-field theory.
